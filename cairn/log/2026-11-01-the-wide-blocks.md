# The wide blocks

Took over from the handoff of the-wide-blocks: 506 lines, ahead of encodify 1.0.2 in 17 of the 18 rows by cycle counts, decoding binary at 64 bytes level with it. The user asked for more speed and less code, free hands, pointed at encodify's qp module and simdutf8, noted that tuning a 1 MiB row broke a 64-byte one and the reverse, suggested decoders specific to rows or blocks, and asked why SIMD was not used. The answer: the passes before kept to safe code the compiler vectorizes with SSE2, because SIMD past a target's baseline needs unsafe code and a CPU token, which the handoff left to the user; and a build of that code for this CPU (`-C target-cpu=native`, AVX-512) gained under 10%, as the walk over each block's special bytes costs the time, not the masks. This pass adds an AVX-512 backend with no branch on the data: by cycle counts, every 4 KiB and 1 MiB row runs 1.07x to 8x faster than the pass before, the accented and binary rows at 64 bytes 1.4x to 3.8x, and the two ASCII rows at 64 bytes are level with it (within 5%, either side by build); every row leads encodify, by 1.4x to 27x. The code went from 506 to 820 lines (tests apart, `cargo fmt`): src/avx512.rs and src/cpu.rs are 281 of them, the dispatch in the other files 33.

What landed:

- `cpu::QuotedPrintableCpu`, a token as base64-codec's: `BASELINE` from `cfg(target_feature)`, unsafe `assume`, `detect` behind a new `std` feature; `QuotedPrintableBackend` is `Portable` or, on x86_64, `Avx512`, which needs AVX-512 VBMI and VBMI2, BMI1, BMI2, LZCNT and POPCNT. `QuotedPrintableCodec` holds it in `cpu`, and `QuotedPrintableDecode` keeps the one of the codec it is built from. The crate denies `unsafe_op_in_unsafe_fn` and `clippy::undocumented_unsafe_blocks` where it forbade unsafe code.
- src/avx512.rs, decoding: blocks of 64 bytes loaded at 0, 1 and 2 bytes on; an escape is `=` and two hex digits, checked by a `vpermb` on their low 6 bits against the bytes themselves, and decoded by two more `vpermb` (high and low nibble values); escapes and soft breaks (`=` LF, `=` CRLF) are masks, and `vpcompressb` drops their digits and breaks once the decoded bytes are blended in; one running past a block carries its lanes into the next. A block stops at the bytes the token steps decide (a malformed `=`, a bare CR, a line break after a blank), and the walk advances 64 bytes a block through a predicted branch: advanced by the data (`read += stop`), the blocks ran one after another, 1.6x slower.
- src/avx512.rs, encoding: chunks of 63 bytes with masks computed once (bytes to escape, line feeds, the CRs they drop, blanks before a line break or the input's end), in three groups of 21 bytes; `vpermb` spreads each byte over three lanes, a nibble blend and a `vpermb` give its hex digits, masked moves put `=` and the CR of a CRLF, the keep mask is `STARTS | pdep(esc) << 1 | pdep(esc) << 2`, and `vpcompressb` packs the group. `pext` moves the literal, escape and line-feed starts to output positions, which give the one soft break a group may need (a literal past the line's room or an escape running past it, before the first line break) and the next column, by `lzcnt` of the line starts: no branch on the data, no misprediction at 1 MiB. Three stores write a group: the vector, the soft break, the vector again past it under a mask. The input's end is one masked partial chunk, with the coroutine's hold logic.
- The user's idea of a decoder per row: on an input of at most 64 bytes with at most 4 bytes to escape, the encoder runs the portable walk whatever the backend (`QuotedPrintableCpu::wide`, one 64-byte masked load): encoding ASCII at 64 bytes went from 2.18 to 1.22 cycles a byte under AVX-512. The same check in the decoder cost more than it saved, and was left out.
- `ROOM` from 160 to 200 bytes, what three groups touch past a chunk's start, so that a chunk takes one room check: it is also the encoder's edge queue and the slack in `encoded_len_max`.
- Decoding, both backends: `tokens` counts the trailing blanks after each blocks call, where the portable blocks counted them every block, and the portable blocks read the byte before a line break from the input; the portable decoding rows run up to 1.16x faster (ASCII at 1 MiB from 2.32 to 2.00 cycles a byte), level on binary at 64 bytes. The pad is filled for the portable blocks only.
- Tests: every test runs under every backend the CPU has (`cpu::available`), and the frozen corpus keeps its hash under each. benches/compare.rs takes `QuotedPrintableCpu::detect` and needs the `std` feature.

Tried and left out:

- Decoding: masked loads for every block, one path and 6 lines fewer: 10% to 20% slower. The encoder's density check (inputs of up to 128 bytes with at most 4 special bytes to the portable blocks): about 50 instructions a call, a loss for accented and binary at 64 bytes. The token steps split into a function per backend, so that the AVX-512 path zeroes no pad: within noise, 12 lines more.
- Encoding: one group of 21 bytes per load, with its own masks: 120 to 150 instructions a group, ASCII slower than the pass before. One loop for whole and partial chunks, lengths and room checked per group: 20% to 40% slower, for 11 lines. A chunk closure called from two places: out of line in text mode, 70% more instructions.

Found: a 64-byte AVX-512 encode with its source and output at the same page offset ran 2x slower than with any other pair (4K aliasing of a group's loads against the stores of the group before): average the offset pairs, and look at pair 0,0 alone. The AVX-512 kernels cost per block, not per special byte, so their 1 MiB rows follow their 4 KiB ones. No SIMD quoted-printable codec turned up to learn from; node-yencode and rapidyenc (yEnc, with an AVX-512 VBMI2 target) and Lemire's simdprune (byte compress without VBMI2) are the closest relatives. Meanwhile the user settled with another session that every SIMD codec takes the shared `simd_cpu::SimdCpu` (simd-cpu's log 2026-11-01-a-simd-cpu-token): this crate moves to it next (the change a-shared-cpu-token, which that session wrote here); that session found the detection here checked no BMI1, which the kernels enable, now fixed.

Criterion, the median of three full runs (64 bytes to 10 MiB) against the published medians of the pass before, in GiB/s: encoding ascii at 64 bytes, 4 KiB, 1 MiB and 10 MiB 3.77, 3.99, 1.92, 1.72 before and 3.62, 4.26, 3.53, 4.37 now; accented 1.39, 1.61, 0.92, 0.95 and 2.05, 4.37, 4.44, 4.40; binary 1.21, 1.33, 0.86, 0.91 and 2.68, 5.96, 5.53, 4.23. Decoding ascii 2.49, 4.90, 1.88, 1.84 and 3.42, 9.85, 10.11, 9.81; accented 1.29, 1.65, 0.92, 0.91 and 3.43, 6.81, 6.96, 7.13; binary 0.80, 0.89, 0.65, 0.64 and 2.54, 4.65, 4.76, 4.67. Every cell leads encodify, by 1.24x (decoding ascii at 64 bytes) to 27x (encoding binary at 1 MiB). Encoding ascii at 64 bytes reads 4% under the pass before (3.55 to 3.66 GiB/s over the runs), level by cycle counts. The cells of this tree moved by up to 10% between runs at 64 bytes and 4 KiB, where those of the pass before moved by up to 2x, and by up to 30% at 1 MiB and 10 MiB; a one-second incremental build ran during the first run.

Checked: the test suite in release and debug with all features, and in release with `-C target-cpu=native` (the AVX-512 backend as `BASELINE`); the library without default features; clippy for all targets with `-D warnings`, with all and with no features; rustdoc with `-D warnings`; 300,000 differential fuzz bodies against the tree before under each backend; `a_frozen_corpus_keeps_its_output` unchanged under both.

Capabilities moved: backends (ADDED: CPU detection is the caller's, The widest backend the CPU has, Every backend matches the portable one), calls (MODIFIED: Lengths and short buffers, No dependency, no std, One codec for every call).
